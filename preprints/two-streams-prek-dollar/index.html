Two Streams of the Pre-K Dollar: One Flat, One Uneven

How equally First Class Pre-K classroom funding is distributed, 2021–22 to 2024–25
JoonHo Lee and Alison Hooper · August 2026

Abstract

Every First Class Pre-K classroom is funded from two streams: a grant from the state’s Office of School Readiness (OSR), and the “other or additional funds” each grantee assembles to cover the rest. We examine how equally each stream is distributed across the program’s 5,867 classroom-years and $844 million in budgeted dollars. We find that the state’s stream is remarkably flat and has grown flatter still, with its Gini coefficient falling from 0.13 to 0.08 over four years, while the other stream is consistently far more unequal, at two to three-and-a-half times the OSR Gini, a multiple that has widened as the OSR stream flattened. Although one might expect that inequality to fall hardest on the classrooms serving the most disadvantaged children, we observe the opposite: the highest-need classrooms average more other funds than the lowest-need ones, likely because federal Head Start dollars concentrate there, and total budgets end up within a few percent of one another across the entire need spectrum.
