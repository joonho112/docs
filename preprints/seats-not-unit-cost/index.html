Seats, Not Unit Cost: What Twenty-Four Years of Pre-K Growth Bought

State investment, enrollment, and per-child spending in Alabama, 2002–2025
JoonHo Lee and Alison Hooper · August 2026

Abstract

In 2002, Alabama’s state pre-K served 756 four-year-olds on a state investment of $5.7 million; in 2024–25, First Class Pre-K served 24,238 children on $185.4 million. In this brief we examine what those twenty-four years of growth bought, drawing on the inflation-adjusted series in the NIEER State of Preschool yearbooks. We find that growth in state investment is associated with nearly proportional growth in the number of children served: investment rose 32.3-fold and enrollment rose 32.1-fold, two multiples that land within a rounding step of each other, while spending per enrolled child ended close to where it started, at $7,585 in 2002 and $7,651 now.
