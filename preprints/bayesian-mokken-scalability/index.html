A Calibrated Bayesian Bootstrap Confidence Interval for Mokken’s Scale-Level Scalability Coefficient

JoonHo Lee · August 2026

Abstract

Mokken scale analysis condenses the question of whether a fixed set of binary items forms a scale into a single scalability coefficient, and applied practice compares that coefficient with conventional benchmarks as if it were free of sampling error. Standard errors are available, but the symmetric intervals built from them assume a shape that this bounded ratio statistic does not have in the samples where it is actually used, and how often such intervals cover the true coefficient had not been established. We construct a confidence interval by repeatedly reweighting the observed respondents with the Bayesian bootstrap and then widening the result by a small deterministic factor. The widening corrects a measured shortfall in coverage, and its single constant was chosen on training data, validated on held-out data, and locked before the final evaluation existed. In a demanding confirmatory simulation the calibrated interval held its advertised coverage in every scenario family at a negligible cost in length, whereas our implementations of the standard formula-based interval and of two refined bootstrap corrections missed far more often and, for longer scales, frequently failed to produce an interval at all. An application to a public scientific-reasoning scale illustrates the method returning a stable and unfavorable answer. The procedure is implemented in the R package bayesmokken, and we suggest that a reported coefficient near a conventional benchmark should routinely carry such an interval.
