The Seats Already Built: Staffing, Not Construction, Is Child Care's Hidden Capacity

What the child-care ecosystem means for pre-K expansion planning, 2024–2025
JoonHo Lee and Alison Hooper · August 2026

Abstract

First Class Pre-K does not expand into empty space, because every new classroom opens inside Alabama’s broader child-care ecosystem—2,164 licensed facilities holding 117,062 licensed slots for some 294,000 children under five. That ecosystem has a capacity problem of its own, and it is not one that new buildings would resolve. Provider surveys summarized in this project’s November 2025 staffing fact sheet put licensed centers’ staffed capacity at about 74 percent of what their licenses allow, with more than 20,000 unfilled positions statewide, and staffing those existing rooms would open space for roughly 14,000 additional children. For comparison, clearing First Class Pre-K’s entire waitlist would seat 5,243, so in this brief we assemble the published aggregates that describe the setting in which pre-K expansion decisions are actually made.
