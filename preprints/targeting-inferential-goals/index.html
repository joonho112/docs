Targeting Toward Inferential Goals in Bayesian Item Response Models for Estimating Person-Specific Latent Traits

JoonHo Lee and Stefanie A. Wind · August 2026

Abstract

When an item response model is fitted, a single set of scores is usually reported and used for every purpose: individual feedback, rankings, and statements about the group, such as the share of examinees below a benchmark. Two choices jointly determine what such reports contain, the prior placed on the latent trait distribution and the summary used to read scores off the posterior, and we evaluate them jointly against the three reporting goals in Bayesian Rasch and two-parameter logistic models, treating test reliability as an explicit design factor. In a large simulation the appropriate summary was determined by the reporting goal: posterior means performed best for individual scores, whereas the constrained Bayes and triple-goal estimators performed best for describing the group’s distribution, and ranking accuracy depended on test reliability alone. The appropriate prior was determined by the data regime: a flexible Dirichlet process mixture prior recovered non-normal trait distributions substantially better than the Gaussian default when tests were reliable and samples large, cost little when the population was in fact normal, but could worsen individual scores for clustered populations measured by short tests. Reliability governed the size of every gain. The comparison was then repeated for thirteen operational tests from the Item Response Warehouse; the same pattern reappeared, and the conventional default of a Gaussian prior read through posterior means carried up to twice the achievable distributional error. The whole workflow is implemented in the open-source R package DPMirt and two companion packages for prior elicitation and reliability-targeted data generation.
