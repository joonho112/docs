The Geometry of Informativeness: A Bregman-Centroid Diagnostic for Partial Pooling in Multisite Trials

JoonHo Lee · August 2026

Abstract

Multisite randomized trials are increasingly analyzed for site-specific effects: how large the effect was at each site, which sites benefited most, and how the effects are distributed. Recent work has shown that the attainable quality of all three answers is well summarized by a single index, the informativeness of the trial, which compares the between-site variation in true effects with a geometric-mean summary of the within-site noise. Two questions have remained open: why a geometric mean appears, and what a single average conceals. This paper answers both with one geometric observation. The weight that determines how far each site’s estimate is pulled toward the overall average is a reliability coefficient in a projection sense, and informativeness is exactly the centroid of the site reliabilities under the geometry natural for quantities between zero and one; averaging on that geometry’s own scale is where the geometric mean comes from. Because a centroid is a projection, it leaves a residual, and the residual measures how unevenly reliability is spread across sites. We prove that this dispersion is linked exactly to the gap between informativeness and the average reliability that sets benchmark estimation risk, with a direction governed by the shape of the reliability profile, and a Monte Carlo experiment shows that the profile predicts estimator performance where the index alone cannot. The same geometry yields a balanced-allocation design rule, and the usual plug-in estimate of informativeness is shown to be systematically optimistic. The Online Credit Recovery Study illustrates the workflow.
