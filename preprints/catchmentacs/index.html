catchmentACS: Isochrone-Based Catchment-Area Profiles from American Community Survey Data with Margin-of-Error Propagation

JoonHo Lee · August 2026

Abstract

Site-level analyses in education, public health, and planning often require a profile of the population living within a fixed travel time of each site on a list, such as the poverty rate around a proposed preschool classroom or the share of households receiving nutrition assistance near a clinic. In the United States such profiles are constructed from American Community Survey (ACS) tract estimates, which are published together with margins of error that are large at the tract scale. Existing R workflows delineate a drive-time isochrone around each site and interpolate tract values onto it, but the interpolation step returns point estimates only, so the published margins of error are discarded at the point where they are most needed. We describe the catchmentACS package, which assembles drive-time catchment profiles with margins of error carried through every stage, from the tract inputs to the derived rates, and with per-row provenance recording how each number was produced. The paper sets out the estimation framework, explains the design of the package, and works through single-site and multi-site illustrations, all of which can be reproduced without a Census API key or a routing account. A simulation study shows that the intervals the package reports are conservative, and that the site comparisons they support are not fragile to the independence assumption inherited from the Census Bureau approximation formulas. In an illustrative ranking of twenty catchments by poverty rate, about one in five pairwise comparisons could not be distinguished at the 90 percent level, which is information that interpolated point estimates cannot provide.
