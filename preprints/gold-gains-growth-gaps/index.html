Same Gains, Different Levels: What GOLD Scores Say about Growth and Gaps

Classroom-level kindergarten-readiness assessment patterns, 2021–22 to 2023–24
JoonHo Lee and Alison Hooper · August 2026

Abstract

Do children in First Class Pre-K’s poorest classrooms gain as much across the school year as children in its most affluent? We bring the program’s own child assessment records—Teaching Strategies GOLD, which teachers complete each fall and spring—to that question for the first time in this series, aggregating 68,888 student records into classroom-year cells (4,194 in literacy) across three school years. We find two patterns that belong together. Classroom poverty is associated with substantially lower assessed skills at the end of the year, by about 20 scale points in literacy when an all-poverty classroom is compared with a no-poverty one. Yet classroom poverty is essentially unrelated to fall-to-spring gains in every one of the six developmental domains: classrooms where at least three-quarters of students live in poverty gained 50.9 literacy points on average, while classrooms where at most a quarter do gained 51.3.
